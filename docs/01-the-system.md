## Level 1: The Ministry

### Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (MoSJE)
**Budget 2026-27: ₹15,357 crore**

- **What it's for:** Welfare, social justice and empowerment of marginalized sections.
- **How it works:** Sets policy and funds its two departments. It doesn't lend to individuals.
- **Who it serves:** SC, OBC, EBC, EWS, DNT, senior citizens, transgender persons, safai karamcharis, victims of substance abuse, and persons with disabilities.

## Level 3A: Identity-based finance corporations (the "wholesalers")

### How their money works
They get almost no yearly budget. They lend from:
1. **Share capital** the government injected in the past.
2. **Repayments** flowing back from earlier loans.

The budget document lists their **lending plan** as "IEBR" (internal and extra-budgetary resources).

**Reach vs need (derived)**
- SC population: ~20.14 crore (Census 2011), roughly ~4 crore households.
- NSFDC reached **~59,000** loan beneficiaries in FY26. That's about **0.15%** of SC households in a year.

**How NSFDC splits money across states**
- At the start of each year, funds are **notionally allocated to states in proportion to their SC population**.
- On **31 August**, unused allocations are reviewed. By **15 September**, money from non-performing states is **reallocated** to states that are using it.
- **20%** of allocation is reserved for cluster development.
- Target mix: **50% educated unemployed, 40% women, 10% others**.
- Sector mix: **50% agriculture, 40% services, 10% industry**.

**Rules a partner must meet to get funds (the "NPA filter" in the PS)**

| Partner | Rule |
|---|---|
| SCA / CA | Government or bank guarantee in place |
| SCA / CA | **No overdues to NSFDC older than 1 year** |
| SCA / CA | **At least 80% cumulative utilisation** of funds already given |
| RRB | **Net NPA below 15%** in at least 3 of the last 6 years |
| RRB | Net profit in at least 3 of the last 6 years |
| PSB | No overdues to NSFDC at time of disbursement |

**Channel network — now CONFIRMED, primary source (Standing Committee 25th Report, Aug 2026)**
- **38 SCAs + 53 alternate Channelizing Agencies (CAs)**, coordinated by only **4 liaison centres**. The Committee itself says 4 centres are "not sufficient" to monitor 91 partners spread across the country and recommends more be opened.
- Number of alternate CAs has grown from **just 17 in 2016 to 53 today** — added specifically because SCAs are weak or absent in many states.
- **SCAs are under-performing or non-performing in 14 states**, due to what NSFDC's own Secretary called a "weak institutional structure" that has developed "over the last few years."
- **Telangana and Ladakh have not appointed an SCA at all** — NSFDC is still pursuing this with both governments.
- **States that give little or no loan guarantee to their SCA** are a separate, additional problem NSFDC flagged during deliberations — this directly restricts how much an SCA can lend regardless of demand.
- **A real structural gap the Committee found:** NSFDC's newer alternate-channel partners (banks, RRBs, NBFC-MFIs) are **not covered under any Credit Guarantee Scheme**, unlike loans through SCAs. This means banks and NBFCs can't extend large loan amounts through this channel because their exposure isn't secured — the Committee recommended fixing this "without any further delay."
- **Best-performing states** (named by DoSJE as having availed maximum cumulative disbursement *and* contributed their own optimum margin-money equity share): Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Kerala, Punjab, Gujarat, Rajasthan.
- **Best-staffed SCAs** (named as sufficiently staffed for smooth implementation): Chandigarh, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Punjab, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, West Bengal.
- **Fund utilisation** is tracked cumulatively across SCA and Alternate-Channel routes for FY23–FY26; NSFDC provided the Committee a detailed utilisation-percentage/pending-amount table by channel and year (not independently re-verified here — treat the utilisation trend as declining year-on-year based on the Committee's tone, but don't quote exact cell values from this table without re-reading the PDF at full resolution).

**Governance and institutional health — now CONFIRMED, primary source**
- Board: **9 of 15 seats vacant** — only 6 official directors currently in place. The Committee called the Department's defence ("vacancies haven't affected functioning") "incomprehensible," and asked: if vacancies don't matter, why have a 15-member Board at all? It recommends filling every seat immediately and pre-empting future vacancies.
- Staff: sanctioned strength is 70. **21 posts vacant as of 31 March 2026, rising to 27 vacant by 28 July 2026** — vacancies are *growing*, not being filled.
- **DPE Corporate Governance guidelines breached in both FY24 and FY25:** the Board is required to meet at least once every 3 months with no more than a 3-month gap between meetings; the gap between the 2nd and 3rd Board meetings exceeded 3 months in both years. The Audit Committee (required to meet 4 times/year, ≤4-month gaps) met only 3 times in both years, also breaching the gap rule.
- NSFDC's own written submission: **"Our portfolio earns an average annual lending rate of 3.1% per annum... unless we get equity from the Central Government, we may not grow... we are not able to actually meet even the inflation, which runs about at a rate of four or four and a half per cent."**
- Marketing fairs (melas): **79 melas in the last 10 years → only 1,170 beneficiaries benefited → ₹10.5 cr total sales generated** (roughly ₹8,974 in sales per participating beneficiary). The Committee is "not satisfied" with this and wants melas institutionalised on a regular calendar rather than ad hoc.

**Multi-cycle impact evaluation — the fullest picture yet (Standing Committee, Aug 2026, citing NSFDC's own external evaluations)**

NSFDC runs its own third-party evaluation studies on a rolling 3-year state-coverage cycle. The Committee's report lays out four completed cycles plus a brand-new 2025-26 one:

| Metric | 2017-18 (13 states) | 2018-19 (9 states) | 2019-20 / MoSJE study (5 states) | 2022-23 (10 states) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Utilisation for intended purpose | 96.31% | 98.6% | 99.60% | 95.10% |
| Beneficiaries who possess the asset created | 94.92% | 93.00% | 99.50% | 92.9% |
| Beneficiaries who crossed the poverty line (BPL) | 99.88% | 100% | 68.10% | **58.8%** |
| Beneficiaries who crossed *double* the poverty line (DPL) | 91.11% | 2.8% | 25.70% | 40.3% |

*(No evaluation cycle ran in 2020-21, due to COVID-19.)*

**This is the single most important number in the whole doc for the pitch:** the share of beneficiaries crossing the poverty line fell from ~99–100% (2017-19) to **58.8% by 2022-23** — a near-halving in real outcome quality even as the Corporation kept lending. The Committee itself calls this "disheartening," noting the Corporation "has not been able to bring the targeted section of society out of poverty" despite decades of existence.

**Newest cycle — 2025-26 evaluation (11 States/UTs, 5,480 beneficiaries: AP, Bihar, Gujarat, HP, J&K, Karnataka, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Tripura):**

| Metric | Result |

| Beneficiaries who possess the asset created | 93.54% (5,126) |

- The Committee's cross-cycle common findings echo the earlier 2020 evaluation almost exactly: slow processing (in 2016-17, **average time from application to loan receipt exceeded 6 months** — an independent, earlier corroboration of the ~4-month figure found separately), insufficient loan amounts, and difficulty from repeated office visits.
- The Committee also flagged a governance gap with no fix proposed yet: **there is no mechanism for beneficiaries or SC community representatives to advise NSFDC or its SCAs on scheme design** — evaluations are commissioned and read internally, with no beneficiary voice in the loop.

**Ground truth: the only field evaluation of NSFDC (MoSJE-commissioned, 2020)**
- **Who and where:** 3,300 loan beneficiaries (2018-19 loans) in Chhattisgarh, Tripura, Punjab, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra. Surveyed Feb 2020.

*Time to get a loan (the missing number)*
- **Application → sanction: ~3 months on average.**
- **Sanction → first instalment: ~1 month.**
- **Total: ~4 months** from applying to receiving money.
- **Partners take 1 month to 1 year** to use the funds NSFDC gives them.
- The study itself recommended a 3-month cap from application to disbursal, which means that wasn't being met.

*Who the beneficiaries are*
- 89.5% rural.
- 42.1% women.
- 15.3% illiterate, 28.4% primary school, 45% middle school, only 10.4% matric or above.
- 94.6% were unemployed before the loan.

*How they found out and applied*
- **61.1% heard from relatives or friends.** 30.8% from officials, 5.5% from NGOs, only 2.6% from awareness camps.
- **57.2% submitted the application at the gram panchayat**, 15.9% at the partner's head office, 10.3% at its district office.

*Friction*
- **31.4% faced difficulty getting the loan.** Of those:
  - 93.4% struggled to provide security or a guarantee.
  - **77.5% had to make repeated office visits.**
  - 6.4% met indifferent officials.
  - 5.1% had document problems.
- What beneficiaries asked for:
  - 67.7% wanted interest-free loans.
  - 16.5% wanted loans without a guarantee.
  - 15.5% wanted faster processing.
  - 13.8% wanted less paperwork.

*Repayment*
- **89.2% repay regularly, 10.8% don't.**
- On average, 17 instalments paid and 2 defaulted.
- Average EMI: ₹2,798/month (range ₹1,600 to ₹7,720 by state).
- **94.8% repay in cash**, and 81.5% repay at a bank.

*Outcome*
- Average household income rose only **12%** (₹86,119 → ₹90,308/yr).
- 93.8% were above the poverty line after the loan and 6.2% still below. The later 2022-23 evaluation found only 58.8% crossed the poverty line (see above).
- **46.3% needed more credit later. Of those, 34% went to moneylenders.**
- 61.5% said lack of expertise in their chosen activity was their main problem.
- Average loan: ₹93,050.

*Digital adoption*
- **None of the sampled channel partners had adopted the SBMS (Social Benefit Management System) software** that NeGD/MeitY built for this.

*Stated plan vs reality*
- NSFDC's 2020-21 Annual Action Plan targeted **₹1,500 cr for 3.58 lakh beneficiaries**:
  - ₹850 cr to 2.5 lakh people through partners.
  - ₹650 cr to 1.08 lakh people through interest subvention.
- The best year since then is **₹775 cr for 59,002 people** (FY26).

**Fund utilisation by channel partners — ⚠️ the number that cuts BOTH ways**

Lok Sabha response, February 2026. As on **23 January 2026**: **₹7,856.65 cr released, ₹7,673.59 cr utilised, ₹183.06 cr unutilised** — a **97.67% utilisation rate (derived)**.

*Corroboration:* the Standing Committee's own channel-utilisation table (para 4.10) shows a FY26 cumulative total in the same range at ~98.55% utilisation, so the two sources agree. **Treat this figure as sound.**

**Government's own stated reasons for under-utilisation (Feb 2026 Lok Sabha answer) — this is the most useful part:**
1. **Delays in beneficiary selection by State Channelizing Agencies**
2. **Time taken by beneficiaries to complete loan documentation**
3. **Non-receipt of utilisation information from district branches**
4. **Pending utilisation certificates**

> **Why this matters more than the rupee figures:** this is the Government of India, answering Parliament, naming the bottleneck as *selection delay, documentation friction, and missing information flow from districts.* Not lack of demand. Not lack of money. **Every one of the four maps directly onto a feature we are building** (see Doc 5: F4 approval odds, F6 document readiness, F7 officer dashboard and stalled-application queue). This is the strongest possible external validation of our feature choices.

**🔴 HANDLE THE 97.67% CAREFULLY. It contradicts a lazy version of our pitch.**

If we say "NSFDC funds go unused," a judge holding this answer kills us in one question. The accurate framing, which is also the more sophisticated one:

| Layer | Performance | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Money released → deployed by partners | **97.67%** ✅ | The plumbing works. Partners spend what they get |
| Plan → actual lending (FY26) | **80%** (₹775 cr of ₹968 cr) ⚠️ | NSFDC lends less than it planned |
| Fresh equity 2019-20 → 2025-26 | **₹29.6 cr total** 🔴 | The tank isn't being refilled |
| Application → decision (UP GIA) | **37.1% decided** 🔴 | The decision layer is the real jam |
| Beneficiary → above poverty line | **58.8%**, down from ~99% 🔴 | Outcomes are degrading |

**The line to use:** *"The money that reaches a partner does get deployed, 97.67% of it. The failure isn't leakage, it's throughput and targeting. The Government's own answer to Parliament says so: the delays are in beneficiary selection, in documentation, and in information never coming back from district branches."*

**And the ₹183.06 cr still matters at human scale (derived):** at NSFDC's FY26 average loan of ₹1.31 lakh, ₹183.06 cr unutilised is roughly **14,000 loans not made** — about a quarter of everyone NSFDC funded in the whole of FY26 (59,002).

### NDFDC (PwD, 40%+ disability, age 18+) — ✅ now closed with official figures (ndfdc.nic.in, as on 31.07.2026)
- Lends through SCAs, PSBs, RRBs and MFIs, like NSFDC. Credit lines ≤ ₹3L and ≤ ₹8L income (same two-line model as NMDFC).
- **Cumulative since 1997-98 (as on 31.07.2026):** ₹1,939.58 cr sanctioned to 2,84,111 beneficiaries; **₹1,878.67 cr actually disbursed to 2,78,875 beneficiaries** — a 96.9% sanctioned-to-disbursed conversion rate **(derived)**. This replaces the earlier low-confidence secondary-source estimate (~₹1,455.59 cr), which undercounted actual scale by roughly 400 crore.
- **Recent-year trend (year-wise, official):**

| FY | Amt sanctioned | Beneficiaries sanctioned | Amt disbursed | Beneficiaries released |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-23 | ₹120.98cr | 18,655 | ₹121.83cr | 18,655 |
| 2023-24 | ₹127.81cr | 16,504 | ₹123.35cr | 16,504 |
| 2024-25 | ₹143.09cr | 17,649 | ₹134.09cr | 17,649 |
| 2025-26 | ₹95.18cr | 2,830 | ₹79.63cr | 2,830 |
| 2026-27 (part-year, to 31.07.2026) | ₹77.75cr | 4,926 | ₹72.40cr | 4,926 |

*(FY25 beneficiary count of 17,649 is on the same order as NSFDC's FY26 figure of 59,002 — NDFDC runs at roughly a quarter of NSFDC's scale, consistent with its much smaller PwD-eligible population base.)*

- **State-wise concentration is extreme:** Tamil Nadu alone accounts for **₹437.46 cr disbursed to 1,07,203 beneficiaries — over 38% of all NDFDC beneficiaries nationally**, more than the next several states combined. Other large states: Kerala (₹197.48cr / 20,493), Haryana (₹162.49cr / 20,246), Maharashtra (₹171.29cr / 17,584), Uttar Pradesh (₹156.93cr / 18,524), Andhra Pradesh (₹88.58cr / 16,297), Rajasthan (₹123.21cr / 12,200).
- **States with negligible reach:** Arunachal Pradesh (3 beneficiaries total, cumulative), Goa (44), Manipur (50), Ladakh (48), Lakshadweep (212), Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (2) — small-population UTs, but also a sign the channel-finance model reaches unevenly by state capacity, the same pattern seen at NSFDC.
- **Source:** ndfdc.nic.in → Achievements → Year-wise Total Disbursement / State-Wise Total Disbursement (downloaded 25 Sept 2026, "as on 31.07.2026").

### NSTFDC (ST, Ministry of Tribal Affairs)
- Schemes:
  - Term Loan: projects ≤ ₹50L, 90% financed.
  - Adivasi Mahila Sashaktikaran Yojana for ST women: ≤ ₹2L at **4%**.
  - Micro-credit for SHGs: ₹50K per member, ₹5L per SHG.
  - Education loan: ₹10L at **6%**.
  - Margin money for Stand-Up India: 15% of project cost.
- Growth: between 2014 and 2025, sanctions and disbursements more than doubled, reach grew 95%+, and it now covers 28 states/UTs.
- Its own site says **over 2 crore ST families are still not covered**.
- National annual disbursement figure: **not found**.

### NMDFC (minorities, Ministry of Minority Affairs), the useful benchmark
- **Eligibility:** notified minorities with income ≤ ₹3L (Credit Line 1) or ≤ ₹8L (Credit Line 2).
- **Recent volume:** ₹2,347.15 cr disbursed over three years.
- **Cumulative:** ₹9,941.50 cr to 26.66 lakh beneficiaries (Aug 2025).
- **Repayment: 96.26% (term loans) and 99.33% (microfinance)** as of Feb 2025.
- **What helps:** SCAs get a 0.5% interest rebate for timely repayment. Third-party studies find 90%+ of financed units still operational.
- **Funding model:** it's a Centre–State joint venture, with states contributing equity in proportion to their minority population.
- **Why it matters:** same channel-finance model as NSFDC, different outcomes. It's the closest real-world comparison for "what good looks like."

## Level 3B: Caste-neutral schemes for the economically weak

### PM MUDRA Yojana (Ministry of Finance)
- **What:** Collateral-free micro-business loans, up to ₹20L. No caste or income test.
- **FY25:** **4.79 crore loans, ₹5,02,782 cr sanctioned, ₹4,91,788 cr disbursed** (98% of sanctioned) **(derived)**.
- **Cumulative (to Mar 2026):** 52.37 crore loans, ₹33.65 lakh cr disbursed.
- **Who gets it:** ~70% women. **~50% to SC/ST/OBC borrowers.**
- **Mix:** Shishu (≤₹50K) is 78% of loans but 36% of the amount. Kishor is 20% of loans and 40% of the amount. Tarun is 2% of loans and 24% of the amount.
- **NPA:** **9.8%** of outstanding loans at the end of FY25, higher than the MSME average.
- **Rate:** set by each bank, not concessional.

### PMEGP (Ministry of MSME, via KVIC / KVIB / DIC)
- **What:** Bank loan plus a one-time subsidy on a new unit.
- **Subsidy:** General 15% urban / 25% rural. Special categories (incl. SC) 25% / 35%.
- **Limits:** ₹50L manufacturing / ₹20L service.
- **No income ceiling.** Age 18+. Class VIII pass needed only for manufacturing projects > ₹10L or service projects > ₹5L.
- **Cumulative (to FY 2024-25):** **10,18,185 micro-enterprises**, ₹73,348.39 cr in loans sanctioned, **₹27,166.07 cr margin-money subsidy** paid.
- **2021-26 cycle target:** ₹13,554.42 cr outlay for ~4 lakh projects and ~30 lakh jobs.
- **Example single disbursement (June 2025):** ₹300 cr subsidy to 11,480 service units against ₹906 cr of loans.
- **Status for 2026-27:** the approved cycle ended FY26. Budget 2026-27 shows *higher* PMEGP allocations, so it's continuing, but a formal Cabinet approval for 2026-31 wasn't found.
- **Delay point:** subsidy is released only after a **3-year lock-in** and physical verification. A missing EDP training certificate is a common reason it gets stuck.

## Target state: UTTAR PRADESH (locked 26 Sept 2026)

### Why UP, and why it holds up to a judge

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Largest SC population of any state in India** | 4,13,57,608 SC people, **20.69% of UP's population** (Census 2011). That is roughly **20.5% of India's entire SC population (~20.14 crore)** **(derived)** |
| Named by DoSJE as a top-performing state | UP is one of 10 states listed as having availed maximum cumulative disbursement *and* contributed its own optimum margin-money equity share (Standing Committee, Aug 2026) |
| One of the few states with **more than one SCA** | Standing Committee names AP, Maharashtra, Kerala, Gujarat, Manipur, Mizoram and UP as multi-SCA states. Means richer routing logic to demo |
| Its SCA already runs an online system | upscfdc.in has a login, employee directory and contacts module, so we're integrating with something real, not inventing it |
| We're based here | KIET, Ghaziabad. Field verification is possible later if a judge asks |

**The strongest framing, and use this one:** UP is a *best-performing* state by the Department's own assessment. If reach is still this thin here, the picture elsewhere is worse. We are not cherry-picking a broken state to make the problem look bad.

**The number that lands:**
- UP's SC population share entitles it to roughly **20.5% of NSFDC's national lending** (allocation is notionally by SC population).
- NSFDC funded 59,002 people nationally in FY26. UP's proportional share is therefore **~12,100 loans a year (derived)**.
- UP has roughly **83 lakh SC households** (4.14 cr ÷ ~5 per household) **(derived)**.
- That is **~0.15% of UP's SC households reached in a year.** About 1 in 685.

### The SCA: U.P. Scheduled Castes Finance and Development Corporation Ltd (UPSCFDC)
- Incorporated **1992**, CIN `U15320UP1992SGC004091`, state government company, HQ **Lucknow**.
- Website: upscfdc.in. Known pages: `/` `/contacts` `/terms` `/login` `/employees`.
- **The site blocks automated access**, so schemes, forms, document lists and district offices must be collected by a teammate in a browser. Already on the task list, about one hour.
